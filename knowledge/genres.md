## Afrobeat — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Groove-driven, rhythmic, hypnotic, energetic
- **LUFS Target:** −10 to −7 LUFS (integrated)
- **Crest Factor:** 8–12 dB
- **Headroom:** −2 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Wide — percussion spread, horns panned, vocals centered

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Kick | Center | 0 dB | Deep, punchy, 4-on-the-floor or syncopated |
| Snare | Center | −1 dB | Sharp, present (often rimshot style) |
| Hi-hat | L/R slightly | −3 dB | Open hats, syncopated patterns |
| Congas | L/R | −4 to −2 dB | Groove foundation |
| Shekere / shakers | L/R wide | −5 to −3 dB | Constant rhythm texture |
| Bass guitar | Center | −1 dB | Groovy, melodic, sub-heavy |
| Rhythm guitar | L/R | −3 dB | Clean, funky, chopped chords |
| Keys / organ | L/R | −4 dB | Swirling, pads, stabs |
| Horns (trumpet, sax) | L/R (trading) | −3 dB | Sharp, bright, call-and-response |
| Lead vocal | Center | 0 dB | Confident, rhythmic, sometimes chanted |
| Backup vocals | L/R | −5 to −3 dB | Repetitive call-and-response |
| Percussion (agogô, cabasa) | L/R wide | −6 to −4 dB | Groove embellishment |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Kick
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–80 Hz | Boost +2 dB (punch) |
| Click | 3–5 kHz | Boost +2 dB for definition |
| Mud | 200–400 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 5 ms attack, 50 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

### Bass Guitar
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–80 Hz | Boost +1 to +2 dB |
| Body | 100–200 Hz | Keep flat |
| Attack | 1–2 kHz | Boost +2 dB for finger noise clarity |
| Mud | 200–400 Hz | Cut −2 to −3 dB |

**Compression:** 3:1, 10 ms attack, 80 ms release, 3–5 dB GR

### Horns
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Body | 200–500 Hz | Keep flat |
| Presence | 2–4 kHz | Boost +3 dB |
| Harshness | 5–7 kHz | Cut −2 dB if harsh |
| Air | 8–12 kHz | Boost +1 dB |

**Compression:** 3:1, 15 ms attack, 100 ms release, 3–4 dB GR

## Mixing Tips

- **Groove is everything:** The rhythm section (drums, percussion, bass) must lock together. Sidechain compression is common (kick → bass)
- **Layered percussion:** Afrobeat uses 5+ percussion layers. Keep them distinct using EQ carving (each gets a different frequency band)
- **Horns should cut through:** They provide melodic punctuation. Bright EQ, not too much reverb
- **Vocals sit in the pocket:** Lead vocals should be present but not overpowering — they're part of the rhythmic texture
- **Open hi-hats:** Very characteristic of afrobeat. Let them ring, boost 8–10 kHz for sizzle
- **Reverb:** Plate on vocals, room on drums, hall on horns. Moderate amounts
- **Call-and-response:** Pan backups opposite to lead. Creates wide stereo field

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Percussion sounds cluttered | Too many layers in same frequency | EQ carve each layer to its own band |
| Bass lacks groove | Over-compressed or too sub-heavy | Reduce compression, let the transients breathe |
| Horns sound too harsh | Excessive 5–7 kHz | Cut with narrow Q, use ribbon mic emulation |
| Mix sounds too busy | All elements playing at once | Automate levels by section, let some breathe |
| Vocals lost in percussion | Masking in 500 Hz–2 kHz | Cut percussion 500 Hz–2 kHz, boost vocals there |
| Drums lack punch | Too much reverb on drums | Keep drums dry or use short room verb only |

## AMBIENT — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Ethereal, spacious, evolving. Focus on texture, atmosphere, and slow harmonic movement. Wide stereo field, extensive reverb, and gentle dynamics.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −18 to −14 (quiet, dynamic)
- **Crest Factor:** 12–20 dB
- **Headroom:** −6 to −1 dBTP (no limiting typically)

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Pads** | Lush, evolving, layered | Full spectrum, HPF 50 Hz, air shelf | Light comp, heavy reverb (hall/shimmer 3–6 s) |
| **Synth textures** | Granular, glitchy, evolving | Wide range, often filtered | Minimal comp, lots of automation |
| **Field recordings** | Natural, textural | HPF as needed | Gate or volume automation |
| **Bass drones** | Deep, sustaining, subby | 30–80 Hz, smooth sine | Very light comp or none |
| **Percussion** | Minimal, soft, organic | Varies, often HPF at 200 Hz | Light comp, long reverb |
| **Vocals** | Ethereal, wordless, processed | HPF 200 Hz, heavy reverb | Long reverb (3–6 s), delay throws |

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Washes out completely | Too much reverb on everything | Use pre-delay, HPF reverb at 300 Hz |
| Muddy low end | Drones and pads overlapping | HPF pads at 100–200 Hz, let drones occupy sub |
| No dynamics | Mix is too flat | Automate volume, add swells and drops |
| Lacks focus | No anchor element | Keep one element drier and more centered |

## BLUES — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Warm, natural, with live-band feel. Emphasis on guitar expression, vocal emotion, and dynamic interplay. Rootsy, not over-produced.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −12 to −10
- **Crest Factor:** 10–14 dB
- **Headroom:** −1.0 to −0.5 dBTP

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Kick** | Warm, thumpy, not clicky | 60–80 Hz, cut 3–4 kHz | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Snare** | Ringing, fat, roomy | 150–250 Hz body, 3 kHz crack, natural ring | Comp 4:1, room reverb 0.5–1.0 s |
| **Bass** | Warm, fingerstyle or upright | 50–150 Hz, cut 300–500 Hz, some 1–2 kHz for fret noise | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Guitar (lead)** | Expressive, overdriven, vocal-like | HPF 80 Hz, cut 400 Hz, boost 1–3 kHz, presence 4 kHz | Comp 3:1–4:1, spring reverb, analog warmth |
| **Guitar (rhythm)** | Clean or light crunch | HPF 100 Hz, cut 300–500 Hz | Doubled L/R, light comp |
| **Piano** | Honky-tonk or grand, rhythmic | Cut 300 Hz for clarity, boost 2–4 kHz | Light comp, room ambiance |
| **Harmonica** | Biting, mid-forward | Boost 1–3 kHz, cut 400 Hz | Light comp, little reverb |
| **Vocals** | Gritty, emotional, raw | HPF 80 Hz, slight presence 2–3 kHz, not over-processed | Comp 3:1–4:1, natural reverb, minimal tuning |

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Too clean/sterile for blues | Over-processing, too much compression | Let dynamics breathe, use analog saturation |
| Guitar thin or weak | Not enough 200–300 Hz body | Boost 200–300 Hz 2–3 dB |
| Vocals sound stiff | Too much comp or pitch correction | Back off comp 2 dB, less tuning |
| Drums sound fake | Samples instead of room sound | Add room reverb, blend in some ambience |

## BOSSA NOVA — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Soft, intimate, warm. Gentle percussion, nylon-string guitar, subtle bass, and whisper-close vocals. Space, touch, and dynamics over volume.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −16 to −12 (quiet, dynamic)
- **Crest Factor:** 12–16 dB
- **Headroom:** −3.0 to −1.0 dBTP (gentle limiting)

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Snare (rim click)** | Soft, tap, brushed | HPF 200 Hz, subtle presence 3–4 kHz | Minimal comp or none |
| **Shaker/Pandeiro** | Gentle, soft, rhythmic | HPF 500 Hz, boost 6–8 kHz | Very dry, low level |
| **Guitar (nylon)** | Warm, fingerpicked, central to mix | HPF 80 Hz, cut 300–500 Hz, warm mids 1–2 kHz, natural | Light comp (2:1, 1–2 dB GR), minimal processing |
| **Bass** | Acoustic upright, soft, melodic | 50–150 Hz, warm, not aggressive | Comp 2:1–3:1, 1–3 dB GR |
| **Piano** | Soft, gentle chords | HPF 80 Hz, warm mids, slight presence | Light comp, room ambiance |
| **Vocals** | Intimate, close, whispered | HPF 80 Hz, boost 150–200 Hz for warmth, cut 400–500 Hz, presence 2–3 kHz (subtle), very natural | Comp 2:1–3:1, 1–3 dB GR (opto preferred), short plate reverb, intimate |
| **Flute/Sax (if present)** | Soft, melodic, warm | HPF 150 Hz, warm mids, gentle presence | Light comp, plate reverb |

## Signature Elements
- **Nylon-string guitar** is the backbone — captured naturally, gently compressed
- **Soft dynamics** — no aggressive compression, let the music breathe
- **Intimate vocal** — close-miked, breath included, minimal processing
- **Gentle percussion** — brushwork, rim clicks, shaker — never loud
- **Wide stereo** — guitar and piano spread, voice centered
- **Warmth over clarity** — slightly dark, not bright or harsh

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Too loud/aggressive for bossa | Over-compressed, too much level | Reduce all levels by 3–6 dB, loosen compression |
| Guitar too bright | Nylon string over-processed | Cut 3–5 kHz, add warmth 200 Hz |
| Too much low end | Bass too heavy or boomy | Cut bass 2–3 dB, tighten with gentle comp |
| No intimacy | Too much reverb or distance | Reduce reverb, bring vocals closer with presence boost |
| Too sterile/digital | Over-processing | Use tape saturation, reduce EQ boosts, let natural tone through |

## CLASSICAL — Genre Mixing Profile

## Genre Identity
Classical is **transparent, natural, and dynamic**. The goal is to capture the performance and the hall acoustics with minimal interference. The widest dynamic range of any genre.

### Sonic signature:
- **LUFS**: -20 to -16 integrated (very dynamic)
- **Crest factor**: 14-20 dB (extremely wide dynamic range)
- **Spectrum**: Natural, flat. Whatever the orchestra produces.
- **Width**: Natural stereo from orchestral placement.
- **Headroom**: -3 to -1 dB peak. NO compression on master bus.

### Philosophy
**"The mix is the microphone placement."** There is no "mixing" in the traditional sense — the engineer's job is mic selection, placement, and minimal corrective processing.

### Recording Approach

#### Main Array (Primary Sound)
- **Decca Tree**: 3 omnidirectional mics across the conductor's position. The classic orchestral sound.
- **ORTF**: Spaced pair for stereo width. French Radio technique.
- **AB**: Spaced omnis for wide, lush sound.

#### Spot Mics (Support)
- Used only to add clarity to sections that need it
- Should be blended subtly — NEVER soloed
- Sections: Strings 1st/2nd violin, viola, cello, double bass, woodwinds, brass, percussion

#### Room Mics (Ambience)
- Far room mics capture the hall sound
- Crucial for the natural reverb and space
- Can be panned wide for immersion

### Mixing (Minimal Processing)

#### STRINGS (Violins, Violas, Celli, Basses)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | Natural balance | Melody lines clearer |
| EQ | Minimal. HPF at 30-40 Hz | Remove subsonic rumble |
| HPF (violins) | 200-300 Hz | Only if needed for proximity |
| Compression | NONE on individual sections | Maybe 1.5:1 on the string bus at most |
| Pan | As on stage: 1st violins left, 2nd right, violas center-left, celli center-right, basses right |

#### WOODWINDS (Flute, Oboe, Clarinet, Bassoon)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | They should float above strings | |
| EQ | Minimal HPF at 200-400 Hz | Depends on instrument |
| Presence (3-5 kHz) | Natural | Flute can be bright |

#### BRASS (Trumpet, Horn, Trombone, Tuba)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | Powerful but not overbearing | They project naturally |
| EQ | Minimal | Cut 3-5 kHz if harsh |
| Compression | NONE | Brass has its own natural compression |

#### PERCUSSION (Timpani, etc.)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | Impactful but natural | Timpani supports the low-end |
| Compression | None | Natural dynamics |

#### HARP / CELESTA / SPECIAL INSTRUMENTS
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | As needed for the piece | |

#### SOLOIST (Concerto)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Level | Slightly forward of orchestra | Natural prominence |
| Spot mic | 5-10% blend | Just enough for clarity |
| Reverb | Same hall as orchestra | Must sound like same space |

### Master Bus (Classical)

| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Compression | NO bus compression | Preserve every dB of dynamics |
| Limiting | Only if needed for broadcast | 1-2 dB at most |
| EQ | Minimal. Maybe a VERY gentle high shelf | If hall is dark |
| Noise gate | NO | Let the hall breathe |
| Dither | Yes, for 16-bit CD | 48kHz or 96kHz preferred |

### Common Issues

**Too much compression**: The most common mistake. Classical music NEEDS its dynamic range.
**Spot mics too loud**: Should support the main array, not replace it. If you can hear spot mics, they're too loud.
**Hall reverb too dry**: The hall IS the reverb. If it sounds dry, blend in room mics.
**Orchestra sounds narrow**: Use a wider main array (ORTF, AB). Check Decca Tree balance.
**Harsh brass**: Cut 3-5 kHz. Check spot mic level on brass section.
**No low-end weight**: Check double bass and timpani levels. Celli may need slight boost at 200-400 Hz.
**Phase issues**: Check polarity alignment between main array and spot mics. Time-align spot mics.

### Sub-genres:
| Variant | Key differences |
|---------|-----------------|
| Symphony | Full orchestra. Large hall. Established rep. |
| Chamber | Small ensemble (3-10 players). Intimate room. |
| Solo | Single instrument + maybe piano. Close, detailed. |
| Opera | Orchestra + vocalists. Vocals need to project over orchestra. |
| Choral | Voices only or voices + organ. Natural room. |
| Contemporary | Modern techniques, extended range. May use more processing. |

## Corridos Tumbados — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Melodic, narrative, acoustic-electric fusion, bass-heavy, regional Mexican
- **LUFS Target:** −9 to −6 LUFS (integrated)
- **Crest Factor:** 6–9 dB
- **Headroom:** −2 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Wide — guitars panned, vocals centered, percussion spread
- **BPM:** 90–120 BPM (typically 100–110)
- **Key Artists:** Natanael Cano, Junior H, Fuerza Regida, Peso Pluma

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Tuba / Bass | Center | 0 dB | Sub-bass, bajo sexto or electric bass |
| Requinto (lead guitar) | Center | −1 to 0 dB | Bright, melodic, fingerpicked |
| Guitarra rítmica | L | −3 dB | Acoustic strummed chords |
| Guitarra de acompañamiento | R | −3 dB | Second acoustic, same pattern |
| Bajo sexto | L/R wide | −4 dB | 12-string bass guitar, rhythmic |
| Trombón / Brass | L/R | −4 dB | Melodic accents, harmonized |
| Snare drum / Tarola | Center | −2 dB | Rimshot style, on 2 and 4 |
| Kick drum / Bombo | Center | −2 dB | Bass drum, 4-on-the-floor or syncopated |
| Hi-hats / Cymbals | L/R | −4 dB | Sparse, ride cymbal |
| Vocals (lead) | Center | −1 dB | Nasal, emotional, storytelling |
| Vocals (harmony) | L/R | −4 dB | Close harmonies, thirds |
| Trombón / Accordion (if present) | L/R | −5 dB | Regional flavor |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Requinto (Lead Guitar)
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | <120 Hz | HPF at 120 Hz |
| Body | 200–500 Hz | Cut −2 to −3 dB (reduce boxiness) |
| Presence | 2–5 kHz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Air | 8–12 kHz | Boost +2 dB |

**Compression:** 3:1, 10 ms attack, 80 ms release, 3–5 dB GR

### Tuba / Bass
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–80 Hz | Boost +2 to +3 dB |
| Body | 80–200 Hz | Keep flat |
| Pick attack | 1–2 kHz | Boost +1 to +2 dB |
| Mud | 200–400 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 10 ms attack, 60 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

### Vocals
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | <100 Hz | HPF at 100 Hz |
| Body | 150–300 Hz | Boost +1 to +2 dB |
| Presence | 2–4 kHz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Sibilance | 6–8 kHz | De-ess |
| Air | 10 kHz+ | Boost +1 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 5 ms attack, 50 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

## Mixing Tips

- **Requinto is the star:** The lead guitar should be bright, present, and articulate. Boost 2–5 kHz generously
- **Tuba/bass provides sub foundation:** The bass is typically melodic and follows the requinto's harmony. HPF at 40 Hz, keep sub tight
- **Acoustic guitars create width:** Pan two acoustic guitars L/R. Slight EQ difference (L brighter, R warmer)
- **Vocals are storytelling focused:** Keep vocals clear and forward. Slight reverb (plate 1.5 s) for space. Doubling on hooks
- **Brass adds punch:** Trombón or brass section should hit hard on accents. Short reverb (room 0.8 s)
- **Rhythm section (tarola, bombo):** The tarola (snare) should be sharp with rimshot tone. Bombo (kick) is sub-heavy
- **Reverb:** Plate on vocals (1.5 s), room on drums (0.8 s), hall on brass (1.8 s)
- **Master bus:** Limiter at −2 dB (−3 to −5 dB GR). Let transients breathe — don't over-compress

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Requinto sounds boxy | Too much 200–500 Hz | Cut 300–500 Hz by 3–4 dB |
| Vocals get buried in brass | Brass masking vocal frequencies | Cut brass 2–4 kHz where vocals live, boost vocals |
| Bass is too boomy | Excessive 100–300 Hz | Cut 150–250 Hz by 3 dB, tighten HPF to 50 Hz |
| Mix sounds muddy | Too many acoustic elements in low-mid | HPF acoustic guitars at 150 Hz |
| Snare lacks crack | Not enough 3–5 kHz presence | Boost snare 3–5 kHz by 4 dB |
| Brass sounds harsh | Excessive 5–7 kHz | Cut 5–7 kHz with narrow Q on brass |
| Mix lacks width | Panning too narrow | Hard pan acoustic guitars L/R, brass L/R |
| Over-compressed | Too much GR on master | Reduce master compression to 2–3 dB GR |

## COUNTRY — Genre Mixing Profile

## Genre Identity
Country is **story-driven, warm, and natural**. The vocal and acoustic instruments take priority. Production ranges from traditional (minimal processing) to modern pop-country (polished).

### Sonic signature:
- **LUFS**: -10 to -8 integrated (mastered, varies by style)
- **Crest factor**: 8-12 dB (traditional), 6-8 dB (modern pop-country)
- **Spectrum**: Warm, mid-forward. Acoustic instruments in the mids. Vocals clear.
- **Width**: Moderate. Steel guitar and fiddles provide width naturally.
- **Headroom**: -1 to -0.5 dB peak

### Per-Instrument Targets

#### KICK DRUM
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -8 to -4 dBFS | Driving |
| Thump (60-100 Hz) | Warm, round | Not too clicky |
| Attack (2-4 kHz) | Moderate | Natural beater sound |
| Compression | Attack 3-7ms, Release 100ms, Ratio 3-4:1, GR 3-6dB | Punchy, natural |
| Reverb | Short room | Natural ambience |

#### SNARE
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -8 to -4 dBFS | Snappy |
| Body (200-400 Hz) | Full | Fat |
| Crack (3-5 kHz) | Strong | Cutting |
| Compression | Attack 5-10ms, Release 150ms, Ratio 3-4:1, GR 3-5dB | |
| Reverb | Room or plate 1-1.5s | Space |

#### BASS GUITAR
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -10 to -6 dBFS | Root note focus |
| Fundamental (60-150 Hz) | Strong | Walking or driving |
| Finger/pick noise | Clean | Clear note definition |
| Compression | Attack 20-50ms, Release 200ms, Ratio 3-4:1, GR 3-5dB | Even |
| Style | Often upright in traditional, electric in modern | |

#### ACOUSTIC GUITAR
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -10 to -6 dBFS | Rhythmic foundation |
| Body (80-200 Hz) | Warm | Full |
| Sparkle (2-6 kHz) | Bright, strummy | Cut through |
| HPF | 80-120 Hz | |
| Compression | Attack 5-15ms, Release 100ms, Ratio 2-3:1, GR 2-4dB | Gentle |
| Pan | Slightly off-center or L/R with double | |

#### ELECTRIC GUITAR
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Support, not dominate |
| Tone | Clean to mildly driven | Twangy Telecaster is classic |
| HPF | 100-150 Hz | |
| Pan | Off-center or L/R | Support acoustic |

#### STEEL GUITAR / DOBRO / LAP STEEL
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -10 to -6 dBFS | Signature country sound |
| Mids (500 Hz-2 kHz) | Warm, singing | Emotional |
| Reverb | Plate or spring 1.5-2.5s | Atmospheric |
| Pan | Off-center | |

#### FIDDLE / MANDOLIN / BANJO
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Texture and energy |
| Fiddle | Bright, cutting 2-5 kHz | |
| Banjo | Sharp, percussive 2-6 kHz | Roll patterns |
| Mandolin | Choppy, bright | Double stops |
| Pan | Across the stereo field | Creates width |

#### LEAD VOCAL
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -6 to -3 dBFS | Storyteller, center |
| Body (200-400 Hz) | Warm, natural | Twang or smooth |
| Presence (2-5 kHz) | Clear, present | Not harsh |
| Air (8-12 kHz) | Moderate | Natural |
| Compression | Attack 10-30ms, Release 100ms, Ratio 3-4:1, GR 3-6dB | Smooth |
| Reverb | Plate or chamber 1.5-2s | Space |
| Delay | Slap 80-120ms | Classic country slap echo |
| Saturation | Tube, subtle | Warmth |

#### HARMONY VOCALS
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Tight harmonies |
| Pan | Center or slightly spread | Often stacked center |
| Harmony intervals | 3rds mostly | Traditional country harmony |
| Stacking | Often 2-3 part harmony | The country sound |

### Traditional vs Modern Country

| Aspect | Traditional | Modern (Pop-Country) |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| Compression | Less (8-12 dB crest) | More (6-8 dB crest) |
| Steel guitar | Prominent | Replaced by synth pads |
| Fiddle | Central | Optional or absent |
| Drums | Natural, room sound | Processed, sampled |
| Vocal style | Twang, natural | Pop-style, polished |
| LUFS | -12 to -10 | -9 to -7 |
| Reverb | Chamber, plate | Hall, digital |
| Production | Minimal, live feel | Polished, layered |

### Common Country Mix Issues

**Vocal too dry**: Country vocals need some space. Slap delay is traditional.
**Acoustic guitar too boomy**: Cut 200-400 Hz. HPF at 100Hz.
**Steel guitar gets lost**: Make room in the mids. Cut competing elements.
**Mix too bright**: Country should be warm. Cut 5-8 kHz if harsh.
**Fiddle too harsh**: Cut 3-5 kHz. Blend with room mics.
**Harmonies not tight**: Compress harmony bus. Tighten with tuning if needed.

### Reference artists:
- **Traditional**: Johnny Cash, Patsy Cline, Hank Williams, Willie Nelson
- **Outlaw**: Waylon Jennings, Merle Haggard
- **90s**: Garth Brooks, Shania Twain, George Strait
- **Modern**: Chris Stapleton, Luke Combs, Kacey Musgraves
- **Pop-country**: Taylor Swift (early), Keith Urban, Thomas Rhett
- **Alt-country/Americana**: Jason Isbell, Sturgill Simpson, Brandi Carlile

## CUMBIA — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Danceable, rhythmic, with prominent percussion and brass. Accordion and guiro are signature elements. Variants from Colombian to Mexican to Argentine cumbia.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −11 to −8
- **Crest Factor:** 8–12 dB
- **Headroom:** −1.0 to −0.5 dBTP

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Kick** | Deep, rhythmic, danceable | 50–80 Hz body, 2–3 kHz attack | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Snare/Redoblante** | Sharp, marching-style | 200 Hz body, 4–5 kHz snap, tight | Comp 4:1–6:1, tight, dry |
| **Timbales/Toms** | Bright, percussive | HPF 100 Hz, presence 3–5 kHz | Light comp |
| **Guiro/Güiro** | Rasping, rhythmic, upfront | HPF 500 Hz, boost 4–8 kHz | Light comp or gate |
| **Accordion** | Bright, melodic, central | HPF 100 Hz, cut 400 Hz, presence 2–4 kHz | Light comp |
| **Bass** | Deep, simple, danceable | 50–150 Hz fundamental, subby | Comp 3:1–4:1 |
| **Brass/Horns** | Bright, punchy, melodic | HPF 100 Hz, presence 3–5 kHz | Comp 3:1, plate reverb |
| **Maracas/Percussion** | Rhythmic, shaker-like | HPF 500 Hz, boost 6–10 kHz | Gate, dry |
| **Vocals** | Energetic, slightly dry | HPF 100 Hz, presence 3–4 kHz | Comp 3:1–4:1, light reverb |

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Percussion too busy | Too many percussion elements | Pan them out, reduce overlapping frequencies |
| Accordion/brass muddy | Too much low-mid | Cut 300–500 Hz 3–4 dB |
| Mix not danceable | Groove not tight enough | Tighten percussion timing, check kick-bass lock |
| Too bright/harsh | Too much hi-hat and percussion top end | Cut 8–10 kHz on percussion bus 2–3 dB |

## Drill — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Dark, aggressive, minimal, 808-driven, trap-influenced
- **LUFS Target:** −7 to −4 LUFS (integrated)
- **Crest Factor:** 3–6 dB
- **Headroom:** −1 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Narrow center-focused, wide adlibs and FX
- **BPM:** 135–160 BPM (UK Drill ∼140, Chicago/Brooklyn ∼150–160)
- **Sub-genres:** UK Drill, Chicago Drill, Brooklyn Drill

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|

| Range | Frequency | Target |

| Range | Frequency | Target |

| Body | 200–400 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

| Range | Frequency | Target |

## Mixing Tips

- **808 slides are signature:** Use pitch bend automation on 808 — slide up 2–8 semitones at end of pattern
- **Hi-hat rolls are essential:** 1/16 and 1/32 note rolls with velocity variation. Slightly off-grid for human feel
- **Dark atmosphere:** Use minor keys, dark pads, HPF on pads for eerie feel
- **Vocal chain:** Aggressive compression (5:1+), slight distortion for aggression, minimal reverb
- **Adlibs panned wide:** Pan adlibs hard L/R with slight detune (±5 cents)
- **Snare layers:** Layer a clap + rimshot + snare. Process as a group
- **Sidechain 808 to kick:** Light sidechain (30–50% ducking) so kick punch cuts through
- **Master bus:** Clipper → limiter (−6 to −8 dB GR). Heavy limiting is characteristic
- **Reverb:** Short room on vocals (0.8 s), longer on FX (hall 2 s). Keep it dark (LPF 8 kHz)

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| 808 too muddy | Too much low-mid energy | Cut 150–300 Hz, keep sub clean |
| Hi-hats sound robotic | No velocity variation | Vary velocity 50–100%, slight off-grid timing |
| Vocals get lost in 808 | 808 masking vocal frequencies | Sidechain 808 to vocal at 200–500 Hz |
| Mix lacks aggression | Not enough distortion or compression | Increase compression on vocals, distort 808 |
| 808 slides sound sloppy | Pitch bend too fast/slow | Use portamento on synth, automate glide time |
| Snare lacks crack | Not enough 3–5 kHz presence | Boost 3–5 kHz, layer with rimshot |
| Too much sub rumble | 808 sub <30 Hz eating headroom | HPF at 25–30 Hz, or use sub harmonic filter |

## Drum & Bass — Genre Profile

| Pads | L/R wide | −5 dB | Atmospheric, liquid pads |

| Vocals | Center | −2 dB | Often sampled, chopped, or ethereal |
| FX / Atmospherics | L/R wide | −6 dB | Reverses, sweeps, ambient textures |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Kick
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 50–80 Hz | Boost +2 to +3 dB |
| Punch | 100–200 Hz | Boost +1 dB |
| Click | 3–5 kHz | Boost +2 dB |

### Sub-Bass
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Fundamental | 30–80 Hz | Boost +3 to +6 dB (enormous) |
| Harmonics | 100–200 Hz | HPF or cut to leave for kick |

**Compression:** Limiter only. Clipper at −1 dB. Pure sine waves.

### Reese / Neuro Bass
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–80 Hz | Leave for sub — duplicate track |
| Body | 150–500 Hz | Cut −3 dB (clear for drums) |
| Growl | 1–3 kHz | Boost +2 to +4 dB |
| Distortion | 3–6 kHz | Boost +2 dB |
| Air | 8–12 kHz | Boost +1 dB |

**Compression:** 6:1, 5 ms attack, 40 ms release, 6–10 dB GR. Multiband comp on low mids.

### Breaks / Drums
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Kick body | 100–200 Hz | Boost +2 dB |
| Snare body | 200–400 Hz | Boost +2 dB |
| Snare crack | 3–5 kHz | Boost +3 dB |
| Hats | 8–12 kHz | Boost +3 dB |
| Mud | 300–500 Hz | Cut −4 dB |

**Compression:** Bus compression 4:1, 10 ms attack, 50 ms release, 3–5 dB GR. Then limiter on drum bus.

## Mixing Tips

- **Sub-bass is sacred:** Keep it clean, mono, and centered. Sidechain to kick (100% duck, 50 ms release)
- **Break chopping:** Chop breaks into 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 slices. Rearrange, pitch, reverse. Process each slice
- **Reese bass layering:** Layer 3–5 saw oscillators with ±5–15 cents detune. Distort, filter, compress as a group
- **Sidechain everything:** Bass ducks to kick. Pads duck to snare. Create rhythmic space
- **Master bus:** Aggressive limiting (−8 to −12 dB GR on peaks). Clipper before limiter for more loudness
- **Reverb:** Short on drums (room 0.6 s), huge on pads (hall 3–4 s). Atmospheric
- **Liquid DNB:** More melodic, less distortion. Focus on pads + vocals + warm bass
- **Neurofunk:** Aggressive reese bass, heavy distortion, complex drum programming
- **Amen break:** Classic jungle/DNB break. HPF at 200 Hz, compress 6:1, add reverb send

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Sub-bass not felt | Not enough low end or phase issues | Check mono, boost 40–60 Hz, check phase with kick |
| Drums sound weak | Not enough punch or layering | Layer kick with sine sub, layer snare with clap |
| Reese is muddy | Too many overlapping frequencies | Multiband compress low mids, HPF at 100 Hz |
| Mix lacks energy | Too much compression killing dynamics | Use parallel compression, leave some transients |
| Bass doesn't cut through mid-range | Too much distortion, not enough mid presence | Boost 1–3 kHz with bell EQ |
| Break sounds stale | No processing on the break | Chop, rearrange, add reverb, filter automate |
| Too much low-end rumble | Sub frequencies below 30 Hz building up | HPF master at 25–30 Hz |

## Mixing Tips

- **Don't over-polish:** Flamenco should feel raw and passionate. Over-compression kills the emotion
- **Palmas are crucial:** They provide the rhythmic drive. Make them sharp and present

- **Footwork (baile):** The zapateado needs sub-bass thump (60–100 Hz) but don't overdo it

- **Dynamic range:** Preserve it! Flamenco is about dynamics — from intimate to explosive

- **Master bus:** Clipper → limiter (−6 to −10 dB GR). Loud and aggressive

- **Synth melody:** Simple, repetitive, catchy. Often one-bar loop

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| 808 drowns the mix | Too much sub sustain | Cut sustain on 808, use tighter envelope |
| Tamborim is too harsh | Excessive 6–8 kHz | Cut 6–8 kHz with narrow Q |
| Vocals get lost | Too much 808 low-mid masking | Sidechain 808 to vocal at 300–500 Hz |
| Beat lacks energy | Drums too quiet or not compressed enough | Increase drum levels, compress tighter |
| Mix sounds repetitive | No variation in arrangement | Add FX drops, filter sweeps, break sections |
| Hi-hats fight with tamborim | Both in same frequency range | EQ carve: tamborim 200–400 Hz, hats 8–12 kHz |

## GOSPEL — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Lush, powerful, dynamic. Emphasis on vocal harmony, choir, Hammond organ, and live-band energy. Emotional build-ups and dynamic contrast.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −12 to −9
- **Crest Factor:** 10–14 dB (wide dynamic range)
- **Headroom:** −1.0 to −0.5 dBTP

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Kick** | Round, warm, felt | 60–80 Hz, soft attack, minimal click | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Snare** | Fat, ringy, live | 200 Hz body, 3–4 kHz crack, room ambience | Comp 4:1, plate/room reverb |
| **Bass** | Warm, fingerstyle or upright | 50–150 Hz, cut 300–500 Hz | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Organ (Hammond)** | Rich, warm, full range | Full spectrum, cut 300–500 Hz if muddy, air shelf | Light comp, rotating speaker effect |
| **Piano** | Grand, bright, and/or electric | Full range, presence 2–4 kHz | Light comp |
| **Guitar** | Clean, warm, chordal | HPF 100 Hz, cut 400 Hz | Light comp |
| **Lead vocal** | Powerful, emotional, dynamic | HPF 80 Hz, cut 400–500 Hz, presence 3–5 kHz, air shelf | Comp 3:1–5:1 (opto+FET serial), plate reverb |
| **Choir (BG vox)** | Lush, layered, wide | HPF 150 Hz, cut 400–500 Hz, presence 3–4 kHz, wide stereo | Spread wide L/R, comp 3:1–4:1, plate/hall reverb |

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Choir muddy in low-mid | Too many voices at 300–500 Hz | Cut 300–500 Hz on choir bus 3–4 dB |
| Choir too wide/weak in center | Too much stereo spread | Keep some choir centered, sides only for doubles |
| Lead vocal buried by choir | Choir too loud or same freq range | EQ carve 3–5 kHz for lead, reduce choir level 1–2 dB |
| Organ muddies everything | Too much low-mid from Leslie | Cut 300–500 Hz 3–4 dB on organ |
| Mix sounds small | Not capturing live energy | Add room verb, keep some natural ambience |

## Grime — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Aggressive, raw, minimal, bass-heavy, MC-driven
- **LUFS Target:** −7 to −4 LUFS (integrated) — extremely loud
- **Crest Factor:** 3–6 dB
- **Headroom:** −1 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Narrow to moderate — focused center, with wide FX stabs
- **BPM:** 130–150 BPM (typically 140)

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Kick | Center | 0 dB | Hard, punchy, often distorted |
| Snare / Rimshot | Center | −1 dB | Sharp, rimshot style or 808 clap |
| Hi-hats | L/R | −4 dB | Fast rolls, triplets, syncopated |
| Bass (sub) | Center | 0 dB | Reese, wobble, or pure sine 808 style |
| Bass (mid) | Center | −3 dB | Growl, square wave, distorted |
| Synth stabs | Center/L | −3 dB | Square or saw, aggressive, reese-style |
| Pad / atmosphere | L/R | −6 dB | Dark, sparse, minor |
| FX (sweeps, sirens) | L/R wide | −7 to −5 dB | Dub sirens, filter sweeps |
| MC / Vocals | Center | −1 dB | Aggressive, fast flow, upfront |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Kick
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Punch | 100–150 Hz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Click | 3–5 kHz | Boost +2 to +3 dB |
| Distortion | — | Subtle saturation for aggression |

**Compression:** 6:1, 3 ms attack, 60 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

### Bass
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–80 Hz | Boost +4 to +6 dB |
| Growl | 100–300 Hz | Boost +2 dB (if Reese/wobble) |
| Distortion | 1–4 kHz | Add for mid-range bass presence |
| Mud | 200–400 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

**Sidechain:** Kick → bass, 50–80% ducking, fast release (30–50 ms)

### MC / Vocals
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | <100 Hz | HPF at 100 Hz |
| Body | 150–300 Hz | Boost +2 dB (warmth) |
| Presence | 2–4 kHz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Sibilance | 6–8 kHz | De-ess |
| Air | 10 kHz+ | Boost +1 dB |

**Compression:** 5:1, 5 ms attack, 40 ms release, 5–8 dB GR. Aggressive.

## Mixing Tips

- **Minimalism is key:** Grime is sparse. Give each element its own space. Don't clutter
- **Bass + kick relationship:** They must lock. Sidechain aggressively. 808-style bass carries the track
- **MC is front and center:** Vocals should be loud, dry, and aggressive. Minimal reverb
- **Stabs hit hard:** Short, punchy synth stabs with fast compressor. Cut through by boosting 1–3 kHz
- **Distortion on bass:** Subtle waveshaping or overdrive on bass for mid-range aggression
- **Hi-hat rolls:** 1/16 and 1/32 notes with velocity variation, slightly panned
- **Dub FX:** Sirens, echo, reverb throws for atmosphere. Use sends, not inserts
- **Master bus:** Aggressive limiting (−6 to −10 dB GR). Clipper before limiter
- **Reverb:** Short on vocals (plate 1.2 s), long on FX (hall 2.5 s). Keep it dark

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Bass is weak / not felt | Too much sub below 30 Hz | Boost 50–70 Hz, HPF at 25 Hz |
| Vocals get buried | Too much bass energy | Sidechain bass to vocals, or carve 200–300 Hz out of bass |
| Mix sounds empty | Too sparse, no atmosphere | Add pads or atmosphere layer (dark, filtered) |
| Stabs are too harsh | Excessive 3–6 kHz | Cut 4–6 kHz with narrow Q |
| Drums lack aggression | Not compressed enough | Increase compression ratio to 6–8:1, boost distortion |
| Hi-hats sound robotic | No velocity variation | Vary velocity (50–100%), slight timing offsets |
| 808 rumble eats headroom | Too much low sub energy | HPF at 25–30 Hz, use limiter with high-pass sidechain |

## JAZZ — Genre Mixing Profile

## Genre Identity
Jazz is **natural, dynamic, and transparent**. The goal is to capture the live performance. Minimal processing, maximum natural sound. Wide dynamic range.

### Sonic signature:
- **LUFS**: -16 to -14 integrated (dynamic, quiet)
- **Crest factor**: 10-16 dB (highly dynamic)
- **Spectrum**: Natural, balanced. Warm low-end, present mids, smooth highs.
- **Width**: Natural stereo from instrument placement and room mics.
- **Headroom**: -3 to -1 dB peak. No limiting or minimal limiting.

### Philosophy
**"Mix the room, not the individual tracks."** Jazz is about the ensemble performance. The room sound is crucial. Over-processing kills the feel.

### Per-Instrument Targets

#### KICK DRUM
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Natural, not overpowering |
| Thump (60-100 Hz) | Warm | Not boomy |
| Attack | Soft, natural | No click needed |
| Compression | None or very gentle (2:1, 2dB GR) | Preserve dynamics |
| Mic technique | Kick in + kick out (room) | Natural blend |
| Reverb | Room ambience | Let the room do the work |

#### SNARE DRUM
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Natural |
| Body (200-400 Hz) | Warm | Fat wooden sound |
| Crack (3-5 kHz) | Moderate | Natural |
| Compression | Gentle or none | 2:1 max, 1-3 dB GR |
| Reverb | Room | Natural ambience |

#### RIDE / HI-HAT
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Peak level | -14 to -10 dBFS | Smooth |
| HPF | 300-500 Hz | Clean |
| Character | Washed, natural | The ride cymbal is crucial in jazz |

#### UPRIGHT BASS

| Peak level | -12 to -8 dBFS | Foundation |

#### PIANO
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|

#### SAXOPHONE
| Parameter | Target | Notes |

#### DOUBLE BASS (Arco/Pizzicato)
As above for upright but with more bow texture when arco.

#### DRUM KIT (Overall)
| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Overheads | Primary sound source | Captures the whole kit |
| Room mics | Key to the jazz sound | Blend more room than close mics |
| Compression | Bus compression gentle (2:1, 1-2dB GR) | Glue only |
| Reverb | Room sound from mics | Don't add artificial reverb |

### Mixing Approach

1. **Start with the rhythm section**: Drums and bass first. Get the groove.
2. **Add harmonic instruments**: Piano, guitar. Balance against rhythm.
3. **Horns sit on top**: They should sound like they're in the same room.
4. **Minimal compression**: Jazz lives on dynamics. Over-compression kills the feel.
5. **Room sound is key**: The room mics are more important than close mics.
6. **Gentle EQ**: Broad curves, not surgical. Maintain natural tone.
7. **Reference**: Compare to classic Blue Note, ECM recordings.

### Common Issues

**Too much compression**: Jazz needs dynamics. Gentle ratios (2:1 max).
**Stereo field too narrow**: Use room mics and overheads for natural width.
**Bass too boomy**: Cut 200-400 Hz if boxy. The upright should be warm but clear.
**Horns too harsh**: Cut 3-5 kHz if fatiguing. Check the mic position.
**Not enough room**: Jazz needs to breathe. Blend in room mics.

|---------|-----------------|
| Bebop | Fast tempos, virtuosic solos. "Live in a club" sound. |
| Cool jazz | Relaxed, smoother. West Coast sound. |
| Hard bop | Blues and gospel influenced. More aggressive. |
| Modal jazz | Open, improvisatory. Early fusion elements. |
| Free jazz | Avant-garde, atonal. Intentionally raw. |
| Fusion | Electric instruments, rock/funk influenced. Heavier production. |
| Smooth jazz | Polished, commercial. More compression, less dynamics. |
| Latin jazz | Afro-Cuban rhythms, percussion forward. |

## Jersey Club — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Energetic, bouncy, viral, dance-focused, bass-heavy
- **LUFS Target:** −7 to −4 LUFS (integrated)
- **Crest Factor:** 3–6 dB
- **Headroom:** −1 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Moderate — centered drums, wide vocal chops
- **BPM:** 130–150 BPM (typically 140)

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Kick | Center | 0 dB | 4-on-the-floor, punchy, often syncopated |
| Snare / Clap | Center | −1 dB | On 2 and 4, sharp and layered |
| Hi-hats | L/R | −4 dB | Fast 1/16 or triplet patterns |
| 808 | Center | −1 dB | Sub-heavy, bouncy patterns with pitch slides |
| Vocal sample | Center | −2 dB | Chopped, pitched, R&B/pop snippets |
| Synth stabs | Center/L | −3 dB | Bright, percussive |
| FX / risers | L/R | −6 dB | Sweeps, DJ drops |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Kick
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 50–80 Hz | Boost +3 dB |
| Punch | 100–150 Hz | Boost +2 dB |
| Click | 3–5 kHz | Boost +1 to +2 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 5 ms attack, 60 ms release, 3–5 dB GR

### 808
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub fundamental | 30–60 Hz | Boost +4 to +6 dB |
| Distortion | — | Subtle saturation for presence |
| Mud | 150–300 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

**Compression:** 6:1, 10 ms attack, 80 ms release, 3–5 dB GR. Sidechain to kick.

### Vocal Sample
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | <100 Hz | HPF at 100 Hz |
| Presence | 2–5 kHz | Boost +3 dB |
| Sibilance | 7–9 kHz | De-ess |

**Compression:** 4:1, 5 ms attack, 40 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

## Mixing Tips

- **Bouncy 808 patterns:** Use syncopated 808 patterns with pitch slides. The 808 often follows the kick pattern with variations
- **Vocal chops are signature:** Chop R&B/pop vocal phrases into 1/4 or 1/8 slices. Pitch them up (+2 to +5 semitones). Process with reverb and delay
- **4-on-the-floor kick:** Classic Jersey Club uses a 4-on-the-floor kick pattern with snare on 2 and 4
- **Hi-hat triplets:** Use triplet hi-hat patterns for the signature Jersey Club bounce
- **Sidechain kick → 808:** Light sidechain (40–60% ducking). Let the 808 breathe between kicks
- **Master bus:** Clipper → limiter (−6 to −8 dB GR). Loud and punchy
- **Reverb:** Short room on drums (0.6 s), plate on vocals (1.2–1.8 s)
- **Structure:** Intro (filtered) → Build (add layers) → Drop (full energy) — short and punchy arrangements

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| 808 sounds flabby | Too much sustain | Cut sustain on sampler, add tighter release |
| Vocal chops sound out of tune | Wrong pitch | Pitch shift to key of track |
| Drums lack bounce | Too much compression killing groove | Use faster release (40–50 ms), less GR (2–3 dB) |
| Hi-hats are harsh | Excessive 8–10 kHz | Cut 8–10 kHz, use darker samples |
| Mix sounds flat | Not enough contrast between sections | Filter intro, add build FX before drop |
| 808 overpowers the mix | Too much sub | Boost 50–70 Hz but cut 100–300 Hz, sidechain |

### Genres within Latin

#### SALSA
- Fast, energetic. Tumbao bass, montuno piano, brass hits.
- Clave pattern is the backbone (2-3 or 3-2).
- Mix priorities: Percussion clarity > vocals > brass > piano.
- Brass should hit hard and short. Staccato.

#### BACHATA
- Romantic, guitar-driven. Slower tempo.
- Guitar (requinto) is the lead instrument. Arpeggiated rhythm guitar.
- Bongos, maracas, bass, and vocal.
- Mix: Guitar forward, vocals warm, percussion subtle.
- Reverb: More than salsa. Romantic atmosphere.

#### MERENGUE
- Fast 2/4 rhythm. Heavy on tambora (two-headed drum) and güira.
- Accordion or saxophone melody.
- Mix: Bright, percussive. Tambora and güira drive the rhythm.
- Bass: Simple, rhythmic, on the downbeat.

#### CUMBIA
- Colombian origin. 2/4 rhythm with syncopation.
- Gaita (flute) or accordion melody.
- Bass: Prominent, often following the rhythm.
- Percussion: Timbales, congas, guache (shaker).

#### REGGAETON
(See separate file: reggaeton.md)

### Common Latin Mix Issues

**Percussion too cluttered**: EQ each percussion piece to its own frequency space. HPF what doesn't need lows.
**Vocals buried in percussion**: Cut 2-5 kHz in percussion to make room for vocals.
**Too much reverb reduces energy**: Latin music needs energy. Keep reverb moderate on rhythm sections.
**Brass too harsh**: Cut 3-5 kHz if fatiguing. Brass should punch, not hurt.
**Not enough low-end for bass**: Salsa bass needs to be felt. Boost 80-150 Hz.

- **Salsa**: Marc Anthony, Hector Lavoe, Celia Cruz, Ruben Blades

- **Merengue**: Juan Luis Guerra, Wilfrido Vargas
- **Cumbia**: Los Angeles Azules, Carlos Vives
- **Latin pop**: Luis Fonsi, Shakira, Ricky Martin

## LO-FI — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Warm, nostalgic, imperfect. Vinyl crackle, tape saturation, deliberate low-fidelity. Detuned pianos, mellow drums, and warm, slightly muffled sound.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −14 to −10 (quieter than most genres)
- **Crest Factor:** 8–12 dB
- **Headroom:** −1.0 to −0.5 dBTP

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Kick** | Soft, round, muffled | 60–80 Hz, cut 3–5 kHz | Comp 2:1–3:1, 1–3 dB GR |
| **Snare** | Loose, roomy, vinyl-like | HPF 150 Hz, body at 200–300 Hz | Room reverb, low-fi vibe |
| **Bass** | Warm, subby or upright | 40–100 Hz, smooth, not aggressive | Comp 3:1, gentle |
| **Piano/Keys** | Detuned, warm, often sampled from old records | HPF 80 Hz, cut 2–4 kHz for warmth | Tape saturation, slight wow/flutter |
| **Drums** | Sampled breaks, vinyl crackle | Lo-fi processing: bit crush, LPF at 8–10 kHz | Vinyl sim, tape sim, subtle compression |
| **Vocals** | Soft, intimate, lo-fi | HPF 100 Hz, LPF at 8–10 kHz, slight distortion | Tape sat, subtle reverb |

## Signature Processing
- **Vinyl crackle:** Layer crackle/pop samples at low volume
- **Tape saturation:** Warmth, slight compression, high-end roll-off
- **Bit crushing:** Reduce to 12–16 bit for authentic lo-fi texture
- **LPF:** Low-pass at 8–10 kHz on master for vintage vibe
- **Pitch drift:** Slight pitch fluctuation (wow/flutter)
- **EQ cuts:** Deliberately cut some highs and lows for "cheap speaker" effect

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Too muddy | Too much low-mid from tape saturation | Cut 300–500 Hz 2–3 dB |
| Too dark | LPF too aggressive | Raise LPF to 10–12 kHz or mix in dry signal |
| Lacks energy | Too much saturation flattening transients | Increase attack on compressor, let transients through |
| Gimmicky, not musical | Over-processing | Dial back effects: tasteful lo-fi, not novelty |

## METAL — Genre Mixing Profile

## Genre Identity
Metal is **aggressive, dense, and powerful**. Extremely layered guitars, fast drums, and intense vocals. The mix must be heavy but clear — a challenge due to density.

### Sonic signature:
- **LUFS**: -8 to -5 integrated (compressed)
- **Crest factor**: 4-6 dB (highly compressed)

- **Width**: Guitars hard-panned L/R (double or quad-tracked). Vocals center. Drums wide.

## POP — Genre Mixing Profile

## Genre Identity
Pop is **vocal-forward, polished, and balanced**. The voice is the star — everything serves the vocal. Production values are high: clean, bright, and wide.

### Sonic signature:
- **LUFS**: -9 to -7 integrated (mastered)
- **Crest factor**: 5-8 dB (moderately compressed)
- **Spectrum**: Balanced with slight mid/air boost. Sub presence but not overwhelming
- **Width**: Wide. Guitars, keyboards, and backing vocals create a large stereo image
- **Headroom**: -1 dB peak before mastering, -6 dB during mixing

### Typical arrangement (by spectral allocation):
| Hz Range | Occupied by | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| 20-60 Hz | Kick sub, synth bass | Air movement, felt more than heard |

| 500 Hz-2 kHz | Vocals, guitars, keys, snare body | The "meat" of the mix |
| 2-6 kHz | Vocal presence, snare crack, guitar bite | Clarity and definition |
| 6-12 kHz | Air, hi-hat, cymbal shimmer, reverb tail | Openness and polish |
| 12-20 kHz | High air, brilliance | Sparkle |

## Common Pop Mix Issues & Fixes

### Vocal not cutting through:
1. Check 2-4 kHz presence on vocals — boost 2-3 dB with wide Q
2. Cut 2-4 kHz on competing instruments (guitars, keys)
3. Reduce reverb on vocals (too much washes out clarity)
4. Sidechain compress instruments to the lead vocal

### Mix sounds small:
1. Widen backing vocals and guitars
2. Add reverb sends to create depth
3. Check low-end: kick + bass should create a solid foundation
4. Automation: push certain words or phrases for dynamic interest

### Muddy low-end (kick + bass fighting):
1. Complementary EQ: kick at 60-80 Hz, bass at 100-120 Hz
2. Sidechain: bass ducks when kick hits
3. HPF everything above the lowest needed frequency
4. Cut 200-400 Hz on bass and kick

### Harshness / ear fatigue:
1. Cut 3-5 kHz on harsh instruments by 1-2 dB
2. Check sibilance on the vocal (de-ess at 5-8 kHz)
3. Reduce distortion or saturation on bright elements
4. LPF at 18-20 kHz on the master bus

### Top 5 reference tracks for Pop:
- **"Blinding Lights" - The Weeknd**: Modern pop mixing benchmark
- **"Bad Guy" - Billie Eilish**: Minimalist, sub-heavy pop
- **"Shake It Off" - Taylor Swift**: Classic pop production
- **"Uptown Funk" - Mark Ronson ft. Bruno Mars**: Funk-pop perfection
- **"Levitating" - Dua Lipa**: Modern pop with wide stereo image

### Genre variants:
| Variant | Key differences |
|---------|-----------------|
| Synth-pop | More synth, less acoustic. Wider. More sidechain. |
| Indie pop | Less polished, more dynamic. Guitars more prominent. |
| Power pop | Heavier guitars, more compressed. Drums more aggressive. |
| Electropop | Heavy sub-bass, vocal chops, electronic drums. |
| Acoustic pop | Natural sounds, less compression, room ambience. |
| K-pop | Extremely polished, dense arrangements, heavy layering. |
| Dream pop | Lots of reverb, washed-out vocals, ethereal pads. |

## REGGAE — Genre Profile

## Mixing Character
Warm, deep, with heavy bass and drums, off-beat rhythm guitar (skank), and laid-back vocal delivery. Space and groove are paramount.

## Target Levels
- **Integrated LUFS:** −12 to −9
- **Crest Factor:** 8–12 dB
- **Headroom:** −1.0 to −0.5 dBTP

## Per-Instrument Targets

| Element | Character | EQ Focus | Dynamic Treatment |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Kick** | Deep, round, on 1 (and often 3) | 50–80 Hz body, soft attack | Comp 3:1–4:1, 2–4 dB GR |
| **Snare** | Tight, rimshot style, on 3 | 200 Hz body, 3–4 kHz crack | Short reverb, comp 4:1 |
| **Bass** | Deep, melodic, central to mix | 40–80 Hz fundamental, 100–250 Hz upper register, cut 400–600 Hz | Comp 4:1–6:1, 3–6 dB GR — very present, drives the song |
| **Rhythm guitar (skank)** | Choppy, off-beat, clean | HPF 100 Hz, presence 2–4 kHz, cut 300 Hz | Light comp, spring reverb |
| **Lead guitar/melody** | Melodic, clean or overdriven | HPF 80 Hz, cut 400 Hz, boost 2–3 kHz | Light comp, spring reverb |
| **Keys/Piano** | Choppy, rhythmic (or organ) | HPF 100 Hz, cut 400 Hz | Light comp |
| **Vocals** | Laid-back, warm, slightly behind the beat | HPF 80 Hz, cut 500 Hz, warm presence 2–3 kHz | Comp 3:1–5:1, spring/plate reverb, delay throws |
| **Percussion** | Bongos, shakers, hi-hats | HPF 300 Hz | Dry or short ambience |

## Signature Elements
- **Bass-forward mix:** Bass is the foundation, often louder than kick
- **Spring reverb:** On guitar, vocals, and drums for classic reggae sound
- **Analog warmth:** Tape saturation for vintage vibe
- **Spacious mix:** Leave room between instruments, not overly dense
- **Dub effects:** Tape delay throws, reverb swells, filter sweeps (more on Dub/reggae)

## Common Issues & Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Bass overwhelms mix | Too much low end or too loud | Reduce level 1–2 dB, tighten with comp |
| Skank guitar too thin | HPF too high or too much 2–4 kHz | Lower HPF to 80 Hz, add 200–300 Hz body |
| Vocals don't sit right | Too far forward or too dry | Add spring reverb, slight delay, drop level |
| Mix lacks groove | Too rigid timing | Let musicians breathe, don't quantize too hard |

### Sonic signature:

- **Spectrum**: Sub-heavy (40-80 Hz), warm low-mids, present vocals 2-5 kHz

- **Headroom**: -0.3 to -0.5 dB peak

## Trance — Genre Profile

## Genre Identity
- **Vibe:** Euphoric, hypnotic, building, emotional, driving
- **LUFS Target:** −8 to −5 LUFS (integrated) — loud and compressed
- **Crest Factor:** 4–8 dB (heavily compressed)
- **Headroom:** −1 dB (true peak)
- **Stereo Width:** Very wide — supersaw pads, ping-pong delays, huge reverb
- **Sub-genres:** Uplifting, Progressive, Psytrance, Tech Trance, Vocal Trance

## Instrument Layout

| Instrument | Pan | Level (relative) | Character |
|-----------|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Kick | Center | 0 dB | 4-on-the-floor, punchy, sidechain trigger |
| Clap / Snare | Center | −2 dB | On 2 and 4, layered clap + snare |
| Hi-hat (closed) | L/R | −4 dB | Eighth notes |
| Hi-hat (open) | L/R | −6 dB | Off-beat accents |
| Bass (sub) | Center | −1 dB | Sine wave, sidechained to kick |
| Bass (mid) | Center | −3 dB | Saw or square, filter sweeps |
| Lead synth | Center | −2 dB | Bright, piercing, melodic hook |
| Supersaw pads | L/R wide | −4 dB | Thick, layered, detuned saw waves |
| Arpeggios | L/R | −4 dB | Rhythmic, 1/16 notes, filter automation |
| FX (risers, impacts) | Center/L/R | −6 dB | Sweeps, white noise builds |
| Piano / Strings | L/R | −6 dB | Emotional breakdown elements |
| Vocals (if present) | Center | −1 dB | Ethereal, layered, reverb-heavy |

## Per-Instrument Targets

### Kick
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | 40–70 Hz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Punch | 100–150 Hz | Boost +2 dB |
| Click | 3–6 kHz | Boost +2 dB |
| Sub-bass rumble | 20–40 Hz | HPF or cut −3 dB |

**Compression:** Heavy sidechain — 6:1, 1 ms attack, 100 ms release, 6–10 dB GR (the kick pumps everything)

### Bass
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub fundamental | 40–100 Hz | Match kick's key, sidechain duck 100% |
| Low-mid | 100–300 Hz | Cut −3 dB to avoid mud with kick |
| Presence | 1–2 kHz | Boost +1 dB for tone character |

**Sidechain:** Kick triggers 4:1 compression on bass, 50 ms release, −10 dB gain reduction

### Lead Synth
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Body | 200–500 Hz | Cut −2 dB (clear space for bass) |
| Presence | 2–5 kHz | Boost +3 to +4 dB |
| Air | 10–16 kHz | Boost +2 dB |
| Mud | 200–400 Hz | Cut −3 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 5 ms attack, 40 ms release, 4–6 dB GR

### Supersaw Pads
| Range | Frequency | Target |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sub | <200 Hz | HPF at 200 Hz |
| Body | 200–500 Hz | Cut −3 to −4 dB |
| Swirl | 1–4 kHz | Boost +2 dB |
| Air | 8–16 kHz | Boost +3 dB |

**Compression:** 4:1, 10 ms attack, 60 ms release, 3–5 dB GR

## Mixing Tips

- **Sidechain is the foundation:** Everything (bass, pads, lead) should pump gently to the kick. Creates the signature "breathing" trance feel
- **Supersaw layering:** Layer 3–5 saw oscillators with ±5 to ±15 cents detune. HPF at 200 Hz. Compress together on a bus
- **Build-ups:** Use white noise risers + filter sweeps + reverb throws over 8–16 bars
- **Breakdowns:** Strip to piano/pads/vocals. Huge reverb (hall 3–5 s). Then drop back to full energy
- **Reverb is essential:** Long hall reverbs (2.5–4 s) on pads and leads. Plate on vocals
- **Delay:** Ping-pong 1/4 or 1/8 dotted on leads. Creates width
- **Master bus:** Heavy compression/limiting (−6 to −10 dB GR on peaks). Trance is LOUD
- **Layering leads:** Layer a saw lead with a square wave an octave below for thickness

## Common Issues & Solutions

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Kick loses punch with sidechain | Too much sidechain gain reduction | Reduce SC GR to 4–6 dB, adjust release time |
| Pads sound muddy | Too much low-mid content | HPF at 200–300 Hz, cut 300–500 Hz |
| Lead synth is harsh | Excessive 5–8 kHz | Cut 5–7 kHz with narrow Q, reduce distortion |
| Build-up lacks energy | No white noise layer | Add filtered noise riser + reverb send automation |
| Mix lacks width | Not enough stereo processing | Use chorus on pads, ping-pong delays, stereo spreaders |
| Drop feels weak | Not enough contrast from breakdown | Cut everything except kick during the 1–2 beat before drop |
| Vocals sit on top instead of in | Too dry, not enough reverb | Add hall reverb (3 s, 30–40%), pre-delay 20 ms |
| Sub-bass not felt | Phase cancellation with kick | Sidechain duck 100%, check phase alignment |
